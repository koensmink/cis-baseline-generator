# Microsoft 365 identity and authentication Candidate review

## Decision model

This review is advisory shadow output. It does not change the production classifier or authorize cutover. A mapping is atomic: one control, capability, boundary, threat scenario, attack path, outcome set, scope, role, and enforced sub-boundary. A control may own several such mappings, and each boundary is evaluated independently.

Evaluation scope is part of boundary identity. `tenant:all_resources|all_users`, administrative-role sessions, Intune Enrollment, Exchange Online, SharePoint, Power BI streaming/PUSH datasets, and risk-conditioned sign-ins are not interchangeable. License entitlement establishes availability only; it does not establish deployment.

## Minimum effective boundary sets

| Boundary | Required security effect | Complementary core | Alternatives | Supporting / tuning / risk-adaptive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MFA enforcement | Independent additional factor, enforced scope, bypass resistance | Separate policies may jointly cover disjoint required populations only when their union is proven complete | An authentication-strength grant that actually requires MFA is equivalent for the same scope | Registration, capability reporting, narrower duplicate policy, prompt tuning |
| Phishing-resistant authentication | Cryptographic verifier binding, origin/channel binding, replay/proxy resistance | All three effects are non-substitutable | FIDO/passkey or certificate mechanisms are alternatives only after their binding properties are evidenced | Ordinary MFA and method preference are supporting |
| Authentication strength | Selected minimum strength, enforced scope, rejection below the floor | Selection and policy enforcement must coexist | Equivalent named or custom strength is acceptable if it enforces the same floor | Disabling only SMS/voice is hardening, not a complete floor |
| Session assurance | Reauthentication freshness and protected session continuation, evaluated at the same scope | Both effects are core | Equivalent token/session lifetime enforcement may substitute after explicit equivalence review | Interval tuning is tuning; user/sign-in risk revalidation is risk-adaptive enhancement |
| Session binding | Transfer prohibition, originating-context binding, token replay resistance | The evidence may be delivered by one control but each effect remains atomic | A different cryptographic token-binding mechanism may be equivalent | Transport protection alone is supporting |
| Managed-device trust | Trusted device identity, current device-state assertion, enforcement at authentication | All three are core and deployment-dependent | Compliant-device or hybrid-joined-device admission may be alternatives for an explicitly stated scope | Enrollment, entitlement, inventory, or compliance evaluation without access enforcement is supporting |

## Remaining ambiguities

- The benchmark does not establish whether every tenant uses Power BI streaming/PUSH datasets; 9.1.9 is Candidate only for its explicit feature scope.
- Intune Enrollment freshness has a concrete enrollment-bypass rationale, but the source does not evidence protected session continuation, so it remains unresolved rather than being promoted on attack-path importance alone.
- The benchmark does not contain a complete phishing-resistant-authentication or managed-device-trust boundary in this reviewed set.
- Authentication-strength wording in MFA remediation permits either an MFA grant or a strength grant, but does not identify a uniform strength floor; it is treated as an implementation alternative for MFA only.
